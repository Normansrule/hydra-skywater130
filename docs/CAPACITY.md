# Expansion roadmap, and what each one needs from an FPGA

Generated by `tools/capacity.py` (run it after changing any estimate). Every
"measured" number came out of a tool in this repository or out of the
session-178 place-and-route table; every "est" is an engineering estimate with
its basis stated in the source.

## The conversion, and why it is trustworthy enough to buy a board with

One design, the TT-A v2 tile, mapped three ways in this session:

| mapping | result | tool |
|---|---|---|
| sky130 standard cells | 28,183 cells / 194,647 um2 | yosys + abc |
| Lattice ECP5 LUT4 | 14,360 LUTs, 3,742 flops, 10 multipliers | yosys + nextpnr-ecp5 |
| Xilinx 7-series LUT6 | 7,539 LUTs, 12 DSP48 | yosys synth_xilinx |

So **0.51 ECP5 LUT4** or **0.27 Xilinx LUT6** per sky130 cell, for logic of
this shape. Wide datapaths (the P-256 units, the systolic array) map more
densely than this; register files map worse unless they become block RAM. It
is an order-of-magnitude answer: right for choosing hardware, wrong for
promising a utilisation figure.

## The verdict on the ECP5-85F you are buying


board verdicts for the whole SoC:
  iCE40 UP5K (iCEBreaker)                 5,280 LUT4  needs  3279%  does NOT fit   $69
  Gowin GW2AR-18 (Tang Nano 20K)         20,736 LUT4  needs   835%  does NOT fit   $30, Amazon
  Artix-7 35T (Cmod A7)                  20,800 LUT6  needs   437%  does NOT fit   $99, DigiKey
  ECP5-85F (ECP5 Evaluation Board)       83,640 LUT4  needs   207%  does NOT fit   $99-159, DigiKey
  Artix-7 100T (Arty A7-100T)            63,400 LUT6  needs   143%  does NOT fit   ~$299
  Artix-7 200T (Nexys Video)            134,600 LUT6  needs    68%  fits   ~$599
  Kintex-7 325T (Genesys 2)             203,800 LUT6  needs    45%  fits   ~$1,200

**For the tile and for incremental work, the ECP5-85F is the right board.**
The tile uses 17% of it. Any single expansion below fits beside it, and most
pairs do. That is the loop you will actually run: bring a block across, run it
against real latencies, move to the next.

**For the whole SoC at once, it does not fit** — 173,145 LUT4 against 83,640.
Two ways forward, and the first is better:

1. **Feature-gated builds.** One target per subsystem: dispatcher plus cores,
   or crypto alone, or the motor axes plus CAN-FD. Each fits comfortably, each
   runs against real timing, and the gate is a plan file rather than a new
   board. This is also what the eventual chip wants, since a FEATURES register
   telling firmware which blocks exist is needed either way.
2. **A bigger board, when you need the whole thing in one image.** Nexys Video
   (Artix-7 200T, 134,600 LUT6, ~$599) takes the SoC as it stands at 68%.
   Genesys 2 (Kintex-7 325T, 203,800 LUT6, ~$1,200) takes it at 45% and leaves
   room for the roadmap below.

**Heavy duty — everything on this page at once.** The SoC plus the GPU plus the
safety, robotics and security expansions comes to roughly 620,000 cells, which
is about 166,000 LUT6: Genesys 2 at ~81%, nothing cheaper, and an UltraScale+
board if you want headroom. That is a real cost step, and it is the moment to
ask whether one image of everything is worth it, or whether the gated builds
plus the silicon itself answer the same questions.

## Two things that change the arithmetic

- **The data RAM is free on an FPGA.** 0.93 mm2 of flip-flops on the die
  becomes block RAM: the ECP5-85F has 208 x 18 kbit and the design uses none
  of it today. Every FPGA number here already assumes that swap.
- **ECDSA is the whole problem.** 188,028 cells is 55% of the current SoC and
  95,777 LUT4 on its own — more than the ECP5-85F has. Sharing the three
  `p256_modmul` instances down to one (the same arbitration pattern
  `ecdsa_top` already uses) is worth roughly 22,000 cells, and it is the
  single change that most improves both the die and the FPGA story.


## Engines, measured in sky130 cells

`synth -flatten`, `dfflibmap` and `abc` against
`sky130_fd_sc_hd__tt_025C_1v80.lib` -- the same method as the tile's numbers,
so the comparison is like for like. Synthesis estimates, not place-and-route.

| block | cells | area | vs the v2 tile (194,647 µm²) |
|---|---|---|---|
| TPU, 4×4 INT8 array | 15,115 | 119,562 µm² | 0.61 |
| SIMD, 4 × 32-bit lanes | 12,861 | 96,967 µm² | 0.50 |
| NTT, 4 Barrett butterflies, q=12289 (Falcon) | 8,551 | 70,524 µm² | 0.36 |
| NTT, q=3329 (ML-KEM) | 7,079 | 57,763 µm² | 0.30 |
| NTT, q=8380417 (ML-DSA) | — | not yet measured: `make area-ntt` | |
| operand streamer | 423 | 4,348 µm² | 0.02 |

The NTT is measured per modulus because `HYDRA_NTT_Q` sets the width of
every multiplier; measured on 2026-10-06 with `make area-ntt`.

The three engines together are about 0.29 mm² of cells (at q=12289) against OpenFrame's
roughly 15 mm² of user area. Cell area is not die area: the earlier
system-on-chip run failed global routing because a 10.3 mm² die could not
hold about 14 mm² of cells at 35% utilisation. Plan die area from the
utilisation LibreLane reports, not from these totals.


## The tile after sharing the cost engine (session 181)

| build | cells | area | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| five parallel cost engines | 18,247 | 194,824 um^2 | what failed to place at 4x4 |
| one shared cost engine | 15,061 | **144,505 um^2** | ships |

Both measured with `synth -flatten` and `abc -liberty` against
`sky130_fd_sc_hd__tt_025C_1v80.lib`, so they compare like with like.

DO NOT compare 144,505 against the 245,525 um^2 in a LibreLane harden log:
different tools, different numbers. Scaling the LibreLane figure by the same
ratio predicted about 53% utilisation. **LibreLane measured 66.7%** on the
first complete harden (2026-10-01) -- the estimate was 14 points low, because
scaling a synthesis number by a ratio ignores the buffers, fill and timing
repair that place-and-route adds. Trust the harden run's number, not this
kind of projection.
